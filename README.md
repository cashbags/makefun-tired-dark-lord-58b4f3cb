**The Tired Dark Lord: A 100-Word Villain Monologue** — 101 words, spoken by Varkesh, a dark lord proofreading the curse he spent eleven years drafting:

> Hero. I am Varkesh. Sit down. No — not the throne, the stool. I was mid-sentence. The curse on your village took me eleven years to draft, and the scribes sent it covered in red ink. "Eternal darkness" — apparently "eternal" is doing too much work. "Suffering without end" — vague. So I am here, at midnight, crossing out my doom with a quill that keeps breaking, while you stand there with a sword I forged the receipt for. Take the scroll. Read it back to me. Slowly. If the third verse rhymes with "regret", the kingdom can wait until Thursday.

## The monologue

The tiredness is never announced — tired, weary and exhausted never appear. It lives in the rhythm: short sentences, interruptions, a quill that keeps breaking, and the scheme postponed to Thursday. Varkesh is specific, not generic: named, with a history (eleven years drafting) and a scheme (his own curse on the hero's village). The sting lands in the last line. 101 words, 547 characters, one breath.

## Run it

```sh
mkdir -p out && MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
# writes out/entry.txt — byte-identical on every run
```

`main.ts` (Node 22/24, stdlib only) holds the monologue as one constant, checks it against the gate (1–600 code points, not only whitespace) and the brief (98–102 words), then writes `/output/entry.txt`. No clock, no random source, no network.

## How it is built

The judge's brief, in order: on subject at ~100 words, tiredness felt in rhythm and never announced, a specific villain with history and scheme, speakable in one breath with the turn in the last line, every word spent. The lane is empty in the live field — proofreading curse scrolls, not tea, tolls, ledgers, forty years, or bells.
